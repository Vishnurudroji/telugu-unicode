03 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Why We Kept Our Version

Not every difference is necessarily a conversion error. Some are deliberate decisions to preserve information.

01

Long vowel sign ూ after మ, ఘ and య

The reference uses a different glyph byte for the vowel sign. A second converter uses the same form as our output, and our version converts back cleanly. Without a licensed font to render and compare both forms, we retained the majority form.

Consistency & compatibility
02

Archaic letters ౘ and ౙ

ANU fonts have no glyph for these archaic letters. The reference silently substitutes చ and జ. We preserve the original letter visibly so that users can notice unsupported content instead of receiving a different word.

Preserve original information
03

ANU 6 visarga ః and ధః

The reference outputs a dash for ః in ANU 6 and represents ధః using the same bytes as దః. This makes the two forms indistinguishable after conversion. We use a colon and preserve the distinction.

Avoid information loss
04

ANU 6 sign ౄ

The reference outputs the literal word "null". Our converter passes the sign through instead of replacing it with an unrelated text value.

Safer fallback behaviour

05 / UNDERSTANDING THE RESULTS

Why Do Round-Trip Differences Occur?

Ambiguous legacy byte sequences

Most exceptions involve vowel signs typed independently, without a consonant. Such standalone signs do not normally occur in real Telugu text.

Other exceptions involve ఙ్క and ఞ్చ with vowel signs, which occur mainly in Sanskrit loanwords. The షొ forms also share the same ANU 7 bytes as షో in the supplied table.

Key point: A legacy encoding may map multiple Telugu sequences to the same byte sequence. Once this happens, reverse conversion cannot always determine the original sequence uniquely.
✓

Transparent conversion, documented limitations

We document differences instead of hiding them. Our goal is to preserve Telugu text, avoid silent substitutions and make legacy publishing content more accessible to modern software.
